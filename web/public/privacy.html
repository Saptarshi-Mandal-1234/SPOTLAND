Privacy at SPOTLAND

Live location sharing

Location sharing is optional and does not require Google sign-in. Starting or resuming asks for explicit consent and browser location permission. Precise latitude, longitude, accuracy and a server update time are sent to SPOTLAND's Worker and D1 database. Only the latest position is stored.

Anyone with your private link can view that position until the share is stopped or expires after 1, 4 or 8 hours. Share links are read-only; updating or stopping requires the sender's signed anonymous Secure/HttpOnly cookie in the originating browser. A link can be forwarded: send it only to people you trust. No location is added to your Google account. A recipient sees the position and update time, not a name or account identifier.

The link secret is in the URL fragment and sent only in an API authorization header. It is not put in server request URLs, analytics, or local storage. This tab's session storage retains the share identifier, link secret and expiry so you can stop or resume after a reload; it stores no precise location. Closing the tab pauses updates but does not revoke the link. Clearing cookies can prevent you from stopping a link; it still expires.

Stop deletes the latest position immediately when the server confirms it. Expired shares are immediately inaccessible and removed on the next sharing API request or the five-minute scheduled cleanup after deployment. Local development has request-triggered cleanup; it does not run a production cron automatically. Random share identifiers and hashed link secrets are retained without locations for up to 30 days to prevent delayed requests reviving ended links. Anonymous device hashes and cookies expire after 30 days. Hashed network/device counters enforce rate limits and expire automatically. The app does not intentionally log location payloads, link secrets, cookies or raw IPs.

The map uses OpenStreetMap tiles. Tile providers see the requested map area and normal request network metadata. Choosing WhatsApp, SMS, the share sheet or copying a link exposes that link to the destination you choose. SMS cannot be sent silently.

Live updates require the sharing screen to remain open and visible. Going Home pauses updates; reopen the sharing screen and consent again to resume. Background location is unreliable; screen Wake Lock is optional and can fail or be released. Errors are displayed; retries send only a fresh latest position and do not keep a travel history. No cached offline recipient location is displayed after a read failure.

This app does not replace emergency services. Call 112 for urgent help.

SOS and nearby alerts

Emergency contact names and phone numbers stay only in this device’s IndexedDB, never on the server. IndexedDB also holds the pending SOS identifier, private share metadata, expiry and an approximate six-character geohash for retry; no precise position or travel history is stored there. Closing or expiring a share makes its link inaccessible, even if local metadata remains. Remove contacts in the SOS screen; clearing site data deletes device storage but does not by itself revoke an active link.

Nearby sending and receiving each need separate explicit consent. The server stores only an approximate geohash for broadcast. The server fixes the radius at 2 km using approximate cell centers; distances and directions are estimates. Receiving consent expires after one hour; nearby location and subscriptions are cleared on expiry or confirmed opt-out. Alerts expire after one hour; “I’m safe” closes the alert and attempts to stop its separate private share. Keep retrying if closure is not confirmed. Reports and help responses are anonymous device records removed with the alert; three distinct device reports hide an alert, not proof that three different people reported it. Hashed counters limit requests by device and network. Ended random SOS IDs remain without location for up to 30 days to reject delayed creation.

Web Push is optional and needs separate notification consent through the Enable push notifications button. When configured, the server stores the browser subscription endpoint and encryption keys only until nearby consent expires or you turn notifications off. Google, Apple or Mozilla browser push services process subscription and encrypted-message requests, including the configured public operator contact. Notifications contain no precise position, contact names or private share link. Turning push off leaves foreground alerts enabled; turning nearby alerts off removes both. Browser notification permission may remain granted until revoked in browser settings. The private test button sends only to this device, creates no SOS record and broadcasts nothing to other devices. Real delivery remains unverified until the displayed test is checked. Foreground nearby polling runs every 30 seconds while the SOS screen is visible. Android/iPhone delivery and emergency workflows require real-device and legal/privacy review before public launch. Notifications, SMS, assistance and delivery are never guaranteed. SMS/WhatsApp/share-sheet actions expose the private link to the recipients you choose and require you to send the message; carrier charges may apply.

Trip planning

Planning needs no login. Explicit address searches use the existing cached geocoder; calculating sends rounded planned coordinates to routing and weather providers, which may keep request logs under their own policies. No current GPS is requested by the planner. The selected day forecast is shown at the starting point only, when available.

Google sign-in is required to save or reopen trips. D1 stores the trip name, date, mode and ordered stop names, addresses and coordinates, scoped to your account. Unsaved drafts stay in screen memory until Home or refresh. Reopened edits save as new trips, preserving originals. Removing a saved trip deletes it and its stops; provider caches and logs are separate. Trip responses are no-store and are not public share links. Offline drafts/sync are not yet provided.

Offline copies

The production app caches its static shell, fonts, illustrations and screens. It does not cache private API responses, live-share positions, SOS responses or Google credentials. Local storage remembers up to 200 favorite public-place snapshots with their account identifier and the last explored public-place list (up to 500 places). The browsing center is rounded to 0.01 degrees, not a GPS history. Copies expire after seven days and are removed when next read. Cached favorites are read-only and do not prove you are signed in. A verified different account or signed-out session clears the previous collection; confirmed sign-out also clears it. Reconnecting refreshes places and verifies the account before refreshing favorites.

Clear offline place copies removes these local snapshots without changing server favorites, emergency contacts, active SOS or sharing. Browser storage may be unavailable or evicted; offline access is not guaranteed. Street tiles are not downloaded for offline use under the current provider policy. Trip changes and favorite changes need a connection.

Community reviews

Reading reviews needs no login. Publishing, editing, removing and reporting require Google sign-in. D1 stores one review per account per public place: the account identifier, place identifier, rating, plain text, visibility and timestamps. Public readers see the text, rating, dates and a generic Traveller label, not your Google name or account identifier. Visits are not verified. Do not include personal details or contact numbers. Text and ratings are public until removed or hidden; copying by others cannot be undone.

Reports associate a review with the reporting account and a timestamp. Three distinct account reports automatically hide the review and exclude its rating from the aggregate; this does not prove the reports are accurate or that accounts represent distinct people. Operators can hide reviews through the protected moderation API. Hidden reviews remain on the server for moderation and are visible to their author. Removing your review deletes it and its reports. Hashed account/network counters apply daily limits and expire automatically. API responses are no-store; drafts remain only in screen memory. Photo reviews use private Cloudflare R2 storage. Browser uploads are JPEG-normalized to remove metadata; server validation and moderation apply before public display. Owners can delete photos; moderators can hide or permanently delete them. Moderation and public-content/privacy wording require review before launch.

Accounts and other data

Optional Google sign-in stores a Google account identifier, display name and favorites. Session tokens are hashed on the server and sessions expire after seven days; signing out revokes the current session. Google tokens, email and profile photo are not retained. Favorites retain saved public-place details. User-submitted events and venues are moderated; crowd estimates use voluntary reports. Device-only emergency contacts and nearby SOS are described above; push requires separate notification consent and configured operator keys/contact.

Attributions

Optional device usage counters

Off by default. You can enable counters under Usage counters on this device on the home screen. They count visits to home, Explore, venues, trips, events and calendar only. They contain no names, account identifiers, locations, URLs, safety actions or timestamps. Counters stay in local storage on this browser; there is no analytics server, third-party analytics SDK or transmission. Turning the option off deletes them. If storage is blocked, the app reports the failure; clearing site data also removes them.

Photo-review support is implemented and the private R2 bucket exists. Photos are held privately until operator approval; storage quotas may prevent uploads. Upload only photos you have rights to share and avoid personal details.
